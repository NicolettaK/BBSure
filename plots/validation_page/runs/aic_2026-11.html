BBSure AIC Run

BBSure validation run with the AIC residual test: 200 simulated sets

7 November 2026 · N. Krachmalnicoff, L. Vacher, G. Galloni, C. Ranucci, A. Carones

  • Decision tree + profile scheme on 200 simulated SO SAT data sets.
  • 100 CMB + noise realizations (seeds 8000–8099); r log-uniform in [10−3, 0.03], 10 sets with r = 0.
  • Each realization analysed twice: with PySM d9s4 (constant spectral indices, so the true model has no moments) and with one PySM sky drawn among d9/d10/d12 × s4/s5/s7.
  • nside 128, no E modes in the signal, hit-weighted noise (pessimistic goal); six SO SAT bands (LF1–2, MF1–2, UHF1–2).
  • Likelihood: Hamimeche–Lewis on the BB cross-spectra of all band pairs, ℓ = 30–250. Fiducial: the BBPower model with r = 0, Alens = 1 and power-law dust and synchrotron (no moments) at the parameters fitted on PySM d1s1, i.e. the mean of the covariance simulations. Covariance: block-diagonal, from 500 Gaussian simulations of that same model (seeds 5002–5501).
  • Code: BBSure branch bic_residuals (commit 97df39e) with BBPower Bsure. Residual test: AIC (a family is detected if Δχ² > 2k, k = new amplitudes); Resr0: all detected families pruned with r = 0, ordered by ΔAIC, each tried as M1,r then M1,0; comparisons first estimated from the ML fits (PolyChord only if ln Best > −5).
  • Computational cost: PolyChord runs per set (tree and profile scheme, counting the reruns of borderline comparisons and the final fit with r = 0): min 2, max 24, mean 7.5 (tree 2–19, mean 5.6; profile scheme 0–9, mean 1.9). One run takes from about 1 to 20 minutes on 8 MPI processes, depending on the free parameters (8 without moments, up to about 16 with them); the ML fits, the profiles in r and the emcee screenings add minutes per set.
  • r in units of 10−3; pull = (r − rtrue)/σ, with σ the standard deviation of the posterior.
  • Bias on r: mean pull +0.18 ± 0.11 for d9s4 (width 1.10) and +0.56 ± 0.11 for the random skies (width 1.11). With the same CMB + noise, the random skies give r higher by +0.46 ± 0.08 σ than d9s4: the residual bias comes from the foreground complexity, mostly d10s7 (+0.76), d12s5 (+0.75) and d12s7 (+0.74).
  • Coverage: the true r is inside the 68% interval in 55% (d9s4) and 58% (random) of the sets; |pull| > 2 in 7 d9s4 and 12 random sets (about 5 expected per 100).
  • Conclusions: 49 detections / 51 upper limits for d9s4, 43 / 57 for random. Wrong conclusions: 3 d9s4 upper limits below the true r (sets 025, 033, 066) and 2 random (set 016, d9s5: upper limit below the true r; set 000, d12s5: detection with r = 0). No false detection at r = 0 in d9s4 (0/10), 1/10 in random.
  • Sensitivity: every set with r ≥ 0.01 is a detection in d9s4 (29/29); 25/29 in the random skies.
  • Foreground model: 86 of the 100 random sets end with at least one moment spectrum. 13 d9s4 sets adopt a moment that is not in the sky (mostly βs×βs, with ln B between 0.1 and 3.8); 7 of them carry an undecided flag. M1,0 is preferred over M0,r in 15 d9s4 and 9 random sets.
  • Flags: three kinds, set by set in the table. PTE < 0.01: 4 d9s4 and 7 random sets. ≈ (a decision taken on a borderline PolyChord comparison): 27 d9s4 and 24 random. r=0 (a model with r = 0 against M0,r): preferred by a lot (✓✓) in 37 d9s4 and 38 random sets, by a little (✓) in 13 and 8, not preferred by a little (✗) in 4 and 9.
  • Goodness of fit: PTE < 0.01 in 4 d9s4 and 7 random sets; the mean χ²/dof (1.05–1.07) is above 1 also for d9s4, a covariance normalisation issue.
