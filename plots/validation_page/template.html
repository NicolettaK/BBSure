How the pipeline works

  • Start simple. Fit the model without moments (dust and synchrotron with a fixed SED, r free). If its χ² is good and r is compatible with 0 at 1σ, stop with an upper limit.
  • Look for moments in the residuals, first with r at its best fit (Reswr: first order in βd and βs), then with r = 0 (Resr0: also Td, second order in β, dust × synchrotron), so that a moment can show up even when r has absorbed it. A family is a candidate if it lowers the AIC of the residual fit.
  • Keep a moment only if the evidence wants it. Each candidate is fitted with all the parameters free, its spectra compatible with 0 are pruned, and it is adopted if its Bayesian evidence beats the current model (ln B > 0). Clearly worse candidates are rejected from the ML fits without the evidence run; borderline comparisons are repeated with other sampler seeds. After an adoption the search starts again from the new model.
  • Moments instead of r. If a model with r = 0 and more moments is preferred over the current one, the search stops: the Bayesian result stays the 3σ test on the current model, and the case is flagged.
  • Conclusion of the tree. 0 outside the central 3σ of the posterior of r → r detection; otherwise upper limit (95%, r ≥ 0).
  • Profile scheme. The final model of the tree and the moment families found with r = 0 are compared again, each at its own r (minimum of its profile likelihood); the posterior of r from the profile of the winner gives the final conclusion with the same 3σ rule.
  • Flags mark what to look at, without changing the result: low PTE, a borderline decision (≈), a model with r = 0 preferred or nearly preferred (r=0).
